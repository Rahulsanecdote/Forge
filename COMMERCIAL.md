# Commercial licensing

Forge is licensed under the [GNU AGPL-3.0](./LICENSE). For most people that is the
end of the matter: run it, modify it, self-host it, use it for your own business or
your own clients, at no cost and with no obligation to contact anyone.

## When you need a commercial licence

The AGPL's distinguishing feature is section 13: if you run a modified version of
Forge and let other people use it over a network, those users must be able to get
your modified source. That is deliberate. It is also incompatible with some business
models.

You need a commercial licence if you want to:

- **Offer Forge to your own customers as a hosted or managed service** while keeping
  your modifications closed — an agency running Forge for its client roster under its
  own brand, for example.
- **Embed Forge in a proprietary product** you distribute or sell.
- **Ship modifications you cannot publish** for contractual or competitive reasons.

You do **not** need one to:

- Run Forge for your own business, however large.
- Run Forge for clients using an unmodified copy, or one whose modifications you are
  willing to publish.
- Evaluate, fork, learn from, or contribute to it.

If you are unsure which side of the line you fall on, ask — the answer is usually
"you're fine", and it costs nothing to check.

## What a commercial licence gives you

The same code under terms that remove the AGPL's source-publication requirement,
plus a named contact for questions. Pricing depends on how you are deploying it; the
per-seat structure for agencies running multiple client accounts is different from a
one-off embedding licence.

## Contact

hello@getforge.ai — tell us roughly what you are building and how you plan to deploy

## Why this structure

Dual licensing is what makes it possible to develop Forge in the open. The AGPL means
the source stays available to everyone and any improvement made to a network-deployed
fork comes back to the commons. The commercial licence means the work can be funded
by the companies that build businesses on top of it. Neither half works without the
other.
